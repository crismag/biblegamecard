# BibleGameCard Project Bible

## Purpose
BibleGameCard is a biblically grounded collectible strategy card project. It translates the identity, calling, legacy, trials, and defining contributions of biblical people and themes into desirable card-game identities without reducing Scripture to spectacle or inventing doctrine.

## Source-of-truth policy
The repository is authoritative. Structured knowledge, registries, schemas, and reviewed specifications are source material. Prompts, images, layouts, PDFs, and exports are generated artifacts and may be regenerated.

## Core pipeline
1. Registry entry
2. Biblical and theological review
3. Gameplay identity
4. Visual identity
5. Structured context
6. Prompt compilation
7. Image generation
8. Art and content review
9. Card layout
10. QA and release

## Non-negotiable principles
- Biblical identity drives gameplay identity.
- Titles express a defining scriptural contribution, not unsupported biography.
- Creative interpretation must never contradict Scripture.
- Scripture references must be traceable.
- Legendary cards should feel powerful, collectible, and strategically distinct.
- Collector IDs are permanent once released.
- Production order is independent of collector numbering.
- Prototype artwork is reference material, not official release artwork.
- Human review is required before an artifact becomes official.

## Initial release scope
The first production milestone is the 34-card Core Legendary Collection, numbered L001 through L034. Phase 1 establishes the system. Phase 2 creates complete character packages. Later phases generate and review official V2 assets.

## Card information layers
Each card package may contain:
- identity and metadata
- biblical basis
- theological guardrails
- legacy and narrative role
- gameplay identity
- artwork identity
- symbols and visual motifs
- prompt inputs
- compiled prompt
- review checklist
- revision history

## Art direction
The target is a premium platinum-style collectible card presentation with cinematic full-art illustration, integrated overlays, strong silhouette, readable hierarchy, controlled symbolism, and print-ready composition. Character identity must remain recognizable across future cards and media.

## Gameplay philosophy
The initial design defines enough gameplay identity to guide card differentiation and artwork. Full balancing and rules-system design remain separate workstreams. Abilities should arise from a character's calling, decisions, relationships, trials, or legacy.

## Theological policy
- Distinguish explicit Scripture from responsible inference and artistic interpretation.
- Do not attribute divine powers to a person as personal magic.
- Miracles must remain acts of God.
- Avoid sectarian claims unless the project explicitly approves them.
- Use respectful language for God, Scripture, and biblical events.
- Flag disputed interpretations for review rather than silently choosing one.

## Version model
- V0: exploration or draft
- V1: prototype
- V2: first official production release
- V3+: approved remaster or major revision

Content version, prompt version, artwork version, and card-layout version should be tracked independently.

## Numbering policy
Reserved prefixes:
- L: Legendary character cards
- E: Epic cards
- R: Rare cards
- C: Common cards
- V: Verse or Scripture cards
- D: Doctrine, devotion, or discipleship cards

Prefixes and ranges may be extended through an approved registry change. IDs must never be silently reassigned.

## Repository boundaries
### Knowledge
Stable or reviewed source materials: specifications, registries, schemas, theology, gameplay identity, lore, art direction, contexts, and templates.

### Generated
Regenerable outputs: compiled prompts, images, card renders, print exports, web assets, and release packages.

## Approval states
Draft -> In Review -> Approved Specification -> Generated -> Reviewed Asset -> Official Release -> Retired or Remastered

## Change control
Changes affecting numbering, theology policy, card schema, art direction, or production workflow require documentation in the relevant decision log and revision history.

## Definition of Phase 1 complete
Phase 1 is complete when the project constitution, directory architecture, standards, templates, schemas, registries, and tracking mechanisms are available and coherent enough to create every Core Legendary package without redesigning the repository.
