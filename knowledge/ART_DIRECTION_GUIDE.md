# Art Direction Guide

## Target visual identity
Premium platinum-style collectible cards with cinematic full-art illustration, integrated framing, restrained overlays, strong focal hierarchy, and print-aware detail.

## Composition principles
- One dominant focal subject.
- A readable silhouette at card size.
- Clear separation between subject, environment, effects, and interface zones.
- Narrative backgrounds support rather than overpower the character.
- Reserve safe areas for title, collector ID, ability text, and badges.

## Character continuity
Each recurring character should have approved continuity notes for age range, build, hair, facial hair, clothing language, recurring colors, equipment, and distinguishing features. Continuity choices are artistic standards unless supported by Scripture.

## Symbolism
Symbols must be traceable to the character's story, calling, or legacy. Avoid excessive icon stacking, unsupported halos, modern denominational branding, anachronistic equipment, and imagery that makes a servant of God appear to be the independent source of divine power.

## Legendary treatment
Legendary cards should communicate presence, consequence, and distinct identity through staging, lighting, environment, and composition—not merely through uncontrolled glow or visual noise.

## Rendering priorities
1. Face, hands, and anatomy
2. Character silhouette and action
3. Defining narrative symbols
4. Environment and atmospheric depth
5. Decorative micro-detail

## Typography and layout
Artwork generation should normally exclude final embedded text. Text and UI are applied during card layout to preserve spelling, readability, localization, and revision control.

## Prototype policy
Earlier generated images may inform pose, lighting, palette, costume, or composition, but they are references only until explicitly approved as official assets.
