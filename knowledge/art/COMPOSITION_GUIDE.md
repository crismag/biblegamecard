# Composition Guide

## Purpose
Defines composition rules for artwork that must remain readable at trading-card size while supporting later frame, title, rules, and stat overlays.

## Primary rule
One image should communicate one dominant visual statement. Secondary details may enrich the scene but must not compete with the subject, face, hands, or defining action.

## Focal hierarchy
1. Face or primary symbolic subject.
2. Hands, tool, weapon, scroll, instrument, or relational action.
3. Supporting figure or environmental event.
4. Contextual background.
5. Decorative detail.

## Card-safe zones
Artwork specifications must declare anticipated overlay zones. Default assumptions for portrait cards:
- keep the face and identifying silhouette away from the outer trim;
- preserve a calm lower region for rules or title integration when required;
- avoid critical details in corners used by icons, collector marks, or rarity badges;
- extend non-critical background beyond final trim for bleed and crop flexibility.

Exact production dimensions and bleed belong to the layout specification, not the generation prompt alone.

## Subject scale
- Portrait: head and upper torso dominate.
- Hero: three-quarter body with strong silhouette.
- Full figure: used when stance, clothing, or action is essential.
- Environmental: figure may be smaller only when location or event is the primary subject.
- Group: use a clear lead figure and limited supporting cast.

Avoid placing a single character so small that identity disappears at thumbnail size.

## Camera language
### Eye level
Human, relational, grounded, and suitable for wisdom, compassion, conversation, or leadership.

### Slight low angle
Adds resolve, authority, courage, or scale. Keep subtle to avoid superhero exaggeration.

### Slight high angle
Supports vulnerability, isolation, prayer, defeat, service, or environmental scale. Do not diminish dignity unintentionally.

### Over-shoulder
Useful for confrontation, guidance, journey, teaching, or revealed environments.

### Wide establishing view
Use when geography, army scale, crossing, city, temple, storm, or journey is central.

Extreme fisheye, action-camera distortion, and arbitrary Dutch angles are discouraged.

## Balance and direction
Use gaze, gesture, weapon direction, roads, architecture, light, crowds, and environmental movement to lead attention. Characters should generally look or move into available image space unless tension requires the opposite.

## Rule of thirds and centrality
Rule-of-thirds placement supports natural scenes. Central symmetry supports kingship, covenant, worship, judgment, temple order, or iconic legendary presentation. Choose based on narrative meaning rather than habit.

## Depth
Use foreground, subject plane, and simplified background. Foreground elements may frame the subject but should not obscure identity. Depth cues include overlap, scale, atmospheric perspective, value compression, and selective focus.

## Action scenes
- Freeze a narratively meaningful moment, not random chaos.
- Preserve body mechanics and object interaction.
- Limit competing figures.
- Use debris, fabric, dust, water, or directional light to support movement.
- Keep the face or defining silhouette readable where possible.

## Group scenes
Group scenes must identify relationships through spacing, gaze, gesture, hierarchy, and clothing. Avoid crowds of repeated faces, duplicated bodies, and ambiguous limbs.

## Symbol placement
Use one dominant symbol or a small coherent group. Symbols should feel physically integrated: carried, worn, built into the environment, reflected, or compositionally echoed. Avoid floating icon collages.

## Thumbnail test
At approximately 5 cm tall, verify:
- the subject remains identifiable;
- the face or core symbol reads;
- the major value pattern remains clear;
- overlays will not destroy the focal area;
- no secondary detail becomes accidental visual noise.

## Crop variants
Where practical, generate or retain enough surrounding image area for:
- portrait card crop;
- full-art card crop;
- square promotional crop;
- widescreen story or web crop.

The canonical approved artwork should record its native aspect ratio and supported crop zones.
