# Character Design Guide

## Purpose
Defines how biblical characters are visually interpreted while preserving dignity, continuity, historical plausibility, and card-scale readability.

## Design order
1. Confirm scriptural identity and life stage.
2. Identify role, calling, occupation, status, and defining scene.
3. Establish continuity traits.
4. Select clothing, objects, environment, pose, and expression.
5. Validate historical plausibility and theological boundaries.
6. Test the silhouette and face at thumbnail size.

## Identity sheet
Each recurring character should eventually record:
- approximate life stage for the depicted scene;
- face shape and distinguishing features;
- skin tone and weathering appropriate to geography and life experience;
- hair and beard pattern;
- height/build tendency without unsupported extremes;
- dominant silhouette;
- signature garments, tools, or symbols;
- approved colour tendencies;
- prohibited or misleading traits;
- references to approved continuity images.

## Age and life stage
Do not use one timeless portrait for every event. A character may require distinct but related designs for youth, maturity, old age, exile, kingship, imprisonment, travel, or ministry. Age should affect posture, skin, hair, clothing wear, and emotional weight without making the character unrecognizable.

## Faces and diversity
Avoid a single generic face across the collection. Use plausible regional diversity, varied facial structures, noses, brows, hair textures, complexions, ages, and signs of labour. Do not modernize features into fashion-model uniformity.

## Body and posture
- Use believable human proportions.
- Reflect occupation and lived experience rather than bodybuilder exaggeration.
- Communicate authority through posture, gaze, and composition—not excessive size.
- Show fatigue, injury, age, or hardship when appropriate without degrading dignity.

## Hair and beards
Hair and beard choices should be plausible for the character, period, culture, status, and scene. Avoid modern fades, salon styling, fantasy braids by default, and identical long hair for every male figure.

## Clothing
Clothing should express:
- historical and geographic setting;
- role and social position;
- wealth or poverty;
- travel, labour, worship, battle, court, prison, or mourning context;
- wear, repair, dust, and material weight.

Prefer linen, wool, leather, woven belts, cloaks, shawls, sandals, period-plausible jewellery, and documented armour forms. Avoid generic medieval-European costume language unless the scene historically supports it.

## Armour and weapons
Armour must not become fantasy plate armour. Weapons should have plausible scale, construction, and handling. Heroism is communicated through action and conviction, not oversized blades or impossible combat poses.

## Hands and objects
Hands often communicate calling: holding a staff, scroll, sling, harp, net, cup, tool, weapon, or another person's hand. Hand anatomy and object interaction are critical review points. Limit signature props to those that clarify the scene.

## Expression
Choose an expression tied to the narrative beat:
- resolve;
- fear overcome by faith;
- grief;
- wonder;
- repentance;
- wisdom;
- compassion;
- worship;
- righteous urgency;
- relief or joy.

Avoid the same stern heroic stare for every card.

## Poses
Preferred poses are readable, balanced, and narratively motivated. Use action poses only when the action is central. Avoid fashion-model posing, impossible contrapposto, generic arms-wide power poses, and ungrounded levitation.

## Silhouette
At thumbnail size, the character should remain identifiable through at least two of the following:
- head/hair profile;
- garment shape;
- posture;
- signature object;
- environment interaction;
- distinctive negative space.

## Status and royalty
Kings, queens, priests, governors, and wealthy figures may use refined materials and ornament, but avoid default European crowns and palace design. Status should emerge from fabric quality, attendants, architecture, jewellery, posture, and scene context.

## Women in the collection
Women should be designed as narratively active biblical people, not decorative counterparts. Clothing, pose, expression, and composition should communicate courage, wisdom, leadership, faithfulness, grief, service, risk, or influence according to the source material. Avoid sexualization and beauty-pageant sameness.

## Children and youth
Use age-appropriate anatomy, clothing, emotional expression, and vulnerability. Do not place children in adult heroic body proportions or unsafe spectacle unrelated to the narrative.

## Continuity review
Before approval, compare against the character identity sheet and approved references. Differences must be explainable by age, context, clothing, lighting, or viewpoint rather than accidental redesign.
