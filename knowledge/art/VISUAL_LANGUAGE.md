# Visual Language

## Purpose
This document defines the shared visual identity for BibleGameCard artwork. It applies to character cards, events, locations, artifacts, miracles, equipment, story scenes, and promotional imagery unless a later approved module overrides it.

## Core identity
BibleGameCard uses **cinematic biblical realism with a refined painterly finish**. The work should feel premium, emotionally immediate, historically grounded, reverent, and suitable for a collectible universe.

The desired balance is:
- believable human characters rather than idealized mannequins;
- dramatic presentation without fantasy-franchise imitation;
- painterly richness without muddy detail;
- historical atmosphere without claiming archaeological certainty;
- spiritual significance communicated through story, light, expression, and symbolism rather than visual clichés.

## Visual pillars

### 1. Biblical identity
Every image must communicate who or what the subject is within the biblical narrative. Pose, expression, environment, objects, relationships, and atmosphere should arise from reviewed source material.

### 2. Human dignity
Biblical figures are portrayed as real people with age, labour, fatigue, courage, grief, hope, conviction, and responsibility. Avoid glamour that erases humanity or caricature that reduces dignity.

### 3. Cinematic clarity
The scene must read quickly at card size. Use a dominant subject, intentional silhouette, clear focal hierarchy, controlled depth, and purposeful lighting.

### 4. Premium collectibility
Images should reward close viewing through material texture, atmospheric depth, restrained ornament, and scene-specific details. Premium does not mean overfilled.

### 5. Unified world
Characters from different books and eras should feel part of one coherent artistic universe while preserving differences in geography, period, status, occupation, and culture.

## Rendering characteristics
- Semi-realistic anatomy and facial structure.
- Painterly surface treatment with controlled brush texture.
- Natural skin texture; avoid plastic or airbrushed faces.
- Rich but disciplined material rendering for cloth, leather, bronze, wood, stone, dust, water, and fire.
- Strong value separation around the face and hands.
- Atmospheric perspective to prevent background competition.
- Fine detail concentrated near the focal point.
- Print-friendly contrast without crushed shadows or clipped highlights.

## Emotional tone
The collection should feel reverent but alive. Common emotional registers include courage, calling, obedience, lament, hope, wisdom, endurance, worship, deliverance, repentance, and victory. Smiling is appropriate when supported by the scene; constant severity is not required.

## Historical approach
Use broad historical plausibility based on reviewed character context. Do not default all characters to the same generic robe, desert, palace, or armour. Record uncertain choices in the character package and avoid presenting speculative details as facts.

## Fantasy boundary
Allowed:
- cinematic atmosphere;
- heightened but plausible light;
- symbolic environmental echoes;
- ornamental card framing added outside the illustration;
- visual grandeur appropriate to the narrative.

Not allowed:
- magical energy controlled by biblical characters;
- superhero costumes;
- oversized fantasy weapons;
- invented monsters in historical scenes;
- glowing eyes used as shorthand for holiness;
- visual imitation of a recognizable commercial franchise.

## Sacred depiction boundary
God is not depicted as a humanoid character by default. Divine action may be communicated through scripturally appropriate phenomena, environmental response, composition, light, cloud, fire, sound implied through reaction, or off-frame presence. Miracles remain acts of God.

## Image-only rule
Primary artwork contains no embedded card title, rules text, collector number, logo, watermark, frame, or rarity badge unless the asset specification explicitly requests a complete card mock-up. Illustration and layout remain separate production layers.

## Acceptance test
An approved image should answer these questions at a glance:
1. Who or what is the main subject?
2. What biblical identity or moment is being communicated?
3. Is the subject readable at trading-card size?
4. Does the image feel part of the established collection?
5. Is the interpretation respectful, plausible, and free from unsupported claims?
