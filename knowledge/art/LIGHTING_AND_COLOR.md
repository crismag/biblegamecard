# Lighting and Colour

## Purpose
Defines a consistent lighting and colour language that supports biblical narrative, emotional clarity, collection cohesion, and print reproduction.

## Core principles
- Light must describe form before it communicates symbolism.
- The face, hands, and defining object receive the clearest value structure.
- Symbolic light should remain restrained and contextually justified.
- Colour scripts vary by scene but stay within the shared painterly visual language.
- Avoid grading every image with the same orange-and-teal treatment.

## Lighting hierarchy
1. Primary subject readability.
2. Narrative emphasis.
3. Depth separation.
4. Environmental realism.
5. Symbolic resonance.

## Standard lighting families

### Calling
Directional light breaking into a grounded environment; focused, anticipatory, and clear. Use sparingly to suggest attention or transition without depicting controllable supernatural energy.

### Courage and victory
Strong directional light, crisp silhouette separation, lifted midtones, and controlled warm accents. Victory should not erase cost, humility, or dependence on God.

### Trial and lament
Lower-key values, reduced saturation, constrained light, weather or environmental pressure, and visible human vulnerability. Preserve facial readability.

### Worship and devotion
Calm, ordered light with warm practical sources, dawn or dusk softness, or luminous atmosphere. Avoid vague glowing fog as a substitute for meaningful worship.

### Prophecy and warning
High contrast, directional interruption, unsettled sky, stark environmental shifts, or restrained colour contrast. Do not default to lightning and glowing eyes.

### Miracle and deliverance
Use environmental response, scale, movement, reaction, and selective luminous contrast. The effect should point beyond the human character and avoid presenting divine action as personal magic.

### Judgment
Weighty contrast, desaturated surroundings, controlled red or amber accents, storm light, smoke, shadow, or severe clarity. Avoid sensational horror unless the narrative explicitly requires terror.

### Peace and restoration
Open values, natural colour, breathable atmosphere, balanced light, and stable composition. Peace is not necessarily pastel or sentimental.

## Colour roles
- Earth neutrals ground the historical world: limestone, clay, sand, wood, leather, wool, dust, and smoke.
- Blues and blue-greens support water, distance, night, wisdom, calm, or royal material where plausible.
- Reds and warm ochres support urgency, sacrifice, royalty, conflict, and human warmth, but should not dominate every legendary card.
- Greens support cultivated land, restoration, provision, and life.
- Gold is an accent for value, royalty, holiness-associated ornament, or premium framing—not a universal divine glow.
- Purple is reserved for plausible costly textiles, royalty, wealth, or later symbolic use with explicit approval.
- White and pale linen may suggest purity, ceremony, status, or light, but should retain material texture and realistic shadow.

## Character palettes
Recurring characters may receive a restrained palette tendency to aid continuity. This is not a fixed costume uniform. Palette may shift with age, location, status, and narrative condition while preserving one or two recognizable tendencies.

## Value design
- Maintain a readable grayscale composition.
- Reserve the strongest local contrast for the focal area.
- Avoid black void shadows that lose print detail.
- Avoid large clipped highlights that remove material information.
- Use atmospheric compression in distant planes.

## Rim light
Rim light may separate a silhouette but must have a plausible or stylistically coherent source. Avoid constant neon outlines and halo-like rims around every character.

## Fire, cloud, and luminous phenomena
Render scripturally relevant phenomena with physical presence, scale, heat, smoke, reflected light, and environmental reaction. Do not reduce them to decorative particles or generic magical swirls.

## Print considerations
- Evaluate artwork in RGB during generation and in a print proof workflow before release.
- Protect shadow detail and skin-tone separation.
- Avoid relying on extreme fluorescent colour that will not reproduce consistently.
- Check small-size contrast after overlays are applied.

## Review questions
1. Is the face readable?
2. Does lighting support the selected narrative beat?
3. Is symbolism restrained and theologically safe?
4. Does colour distinguish this card without breaking collection cohesion?
5. Will values survive reduction and print conversion?
