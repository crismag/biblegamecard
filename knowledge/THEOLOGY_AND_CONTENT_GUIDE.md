# Theology and Content Guide

## Evidence labels
Every substantive claim used in a card package should be understood as one of:
- **Explicit**: directly stated in Scripture.
- **Supported inference**: reasonably derived from the text and clearly labeled.
- **Tradition**: inherited interpretation or historical tradition, not presented as Scripture.
- **Creative interpretation**: visual or gameplay invention that does not contradict the text.
- **Disputed**: interpretation requiring visible review notes.

## Content rules
- Cite primary passages for a card's defining identity.
- Preserve the distinction between God's action and a person's obedience or instrumentality.
- Do not portray biblical people as independent supernatural sources.
- Avoid invented moral claims, relationships, motives, or outcomes.
- Do not use visual symbols that introduce conflicting religious meaning without review.
- Avoid making physical appearance claims that Scripture does not support; classify them as continuity choices.
- Card text may summarize but must not misquote Scripture.

## Review questions
1. What does the passage explicitly say?
2. What is inferred?
3. What is added for gameplay or visual storytelling?
4. Could the wording imply a doctrine the passage does not teach?
5. Does the ability make the person, rather than God, the source of a miracle?
6. Are disputed interpretations documented?
7. Can each defining statement be traced to a passage or labeled source?

## Character dignity
Characters, including antagonists and morally complex people, should not be caricatured beyond the biblical witness. Sin, failure, repentance, judgment, courage, and faith should be represented with seriousness appropriate to the source.
