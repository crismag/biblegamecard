# Gameplay Design Guide

## Scope
Phase 1 defines gameplay identity and data contracts, not final balance. Numerical tuning and complete rules belong to the dedicated game-system workstream.

## Design principles
- A card's mechanics should arise from the person's biblical identity, choices, relationships, trials, or legacy.
- Every Legendary should have a distinct strategic role.
- Power should create meaningful decisions, not automatic victory.
- Miracles are represented as God's intervention, covenant, calling, answered prayer, or event effects—not personal magic.
- Failure, weakness, repentance, and restoration may be mechanically meaningful where biblically appropriate.

## Ability design test
A proposed ability should answer:
1. Why does this belong specifically to this character?
2. Which passages support it?
3. What decision does it create for the player?
4. What counters or limits it?
5. Does it overlap another Legendary?
6. Does its wording imply unsupported theology?

## Initial role vocabulary
Leader, prophet, judge, king, warrior, priest, apostle, evangelist, intercessor, builder, reformer, deliverer, witness, disciple, matriarch, patriarch, wisdom, endurance, restoration.

## Rules-text policy
Rules text must be concise, testable, and free of narrative ambiguity. Flavor text may carry devotional or thematic language, but mechanics must use controlled terms.

## Balance metadata
Each ability specification should include activation type, cost or condition, target, duration, frequency, synergy, counterplay, and design risk, even when values remain provisional.
