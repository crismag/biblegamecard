# Gameplay Identity

> Design direction only. Numbers and final rules require balancing and separate approval.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| Primary role | Battle Commander / Leader |
| Secondary roles | Tempo, coordinated aggression, objective control |
| Strategic identity | Converts ordered allied participation into pressure against fortifications, then secures territory. |
| Tempo profile | Builds formation, creates a decisive opening, and rewards follow-through rather than solo burst damage. |
| Player promise | “My army advances together, barriers open, and claimed ground becomes inheritance.” |

## Core mechanic families
- **Command/formation:** reward multiple allied roles acting in sequence; avoid treating priests or the ark as combat consumables.
- **Courage:** resist fear, hesitation, or morale disruption when covenant conditions are met.
- **Fortification breach:** “Walls Fall Down” answers defenses after preparation, not as Joshua's personal magic.
- **Crossing/path:** temporarily open a route through terrain or control effects, with divine agency represented by event/support framing.
- **Memorial/inheritance:** place a persistent marker after an objective is secured; reward holding rather than indiscriminate destruction.
- **Covenant accountability:** powerful leadership loses efficiency when the formation breaks faith or acts presumptuously.

## Candidate keywords
`Command`, `Courage`, `Formation`, `Advance`, `Claim`, `Memorial`. These are vocabulary candidates, not locked rules terms.

## Strengths
- Coordinated allied turns and battlefield positioning.
- Pressure against static defenses.
- Fear resistance and objective conversion.
- Durable advantage after legitimately claiming territory.

## Weaknesses and counterplay
- Weaker without allied formation or preparation.
- Vulnerable to disruption before the coordinated action resolves.
- Covenant/accountability costs prevent unconditional conquest.
- Less flexible in solitary duels; not a supernatural damage caster.

## Interactions
- Positive: Israel, priest/support, covenant, memorial, and inheritance systems.
- Distinct from Moses: Moses liberates and controls barriers; Joshua advances, coordinates, and claims objectives.
- Distinct from Caleb: Caleb is an enduring individual claimant; Joshua is a whole-army commander.
- Prohibited: mechanics that make the ark a magical weapon, reward cruelty, target an ethnicity, or attribute God's miracles intrinsically to Joshua.
