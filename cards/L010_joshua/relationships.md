# Relationships

| Person/group | Relationship | Importance and influence | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| The LORD | Joshua is called, charged, accompanied, corrected through covenant consequences, and given the land-entry task. | Primary source of authority and victory; Joshua is servant, never an independent miracle-worker. | Numbers 27:18–23; Deuteronomy 31:14–23; Joshua 1:1–9; 24:29 |
| Moses | Assistant, military delegate, disciple, and commissioned successor. | Shapes Joshua's preparation and publicly confirms succession; Joshua does not replace Moses' unique prophetic standing. | Exodus 17:8–16; 24:13; Numbers 27:18–23; Deuteronomy 34:9–12 |
| Caleb | Fellow scout and faithful dissenter; later receives Hebron. | Their shared trust contrasts with the other scouts; Caleb's distinct inheritance remains his own story. | Numbers 14:6–9,30; Joshua 14:6–15 |
| Eleazar | Priest before whom Joshua is commissioned; partner in allotment. | Marks accountable leadership and priestly consultation rather than solitary rule. | Numbers 27:18–23; Joshua 14:1; 19:51 |
| Rahab | Protects Joshua's spies; Joshua spares her household. | Links Jericho with oath-keeping and mercy. No personal meeting is stated before her rescue. | Joshua 2; 6:22–25 |
| Priests bearing the ark | Liturgical participants at Jordan and Jericho. | Make covenant presence and ordered obedience visible; they are not decorative soldiers. | Joshua 3–4; 6 |
| Israel and its elders/officers | People Joshua leads, instructs, and gathers in covenant. | His leadership is corporate, covenantal, and accountable. | Joshua 1:10–18; 8:30–35; 23–24 |
| Achan | Israelite whose covenant violation is exposed after defeat at Ai. | Demonstrates that Joshua's command does not guarantee victory apart from covenant faithfulness. | Joshua 7 |
| Gibeonites | Treaty partners after their deception; later defended by Israel. | Shows Joshua's failure to seek counsel and Israel's obligation to an oath. | Joshua 9–10 |
| Eastern tribes | Reuben, Gad, and half-Manasseh fulfill their commitment; later face an altar dispute. | Displays shared service, misunderstanding, investigation, and restored peace. | Joshua 1:12–18; 22 |
| Commander of the LORD's army | Mysterious figure Joshua encounters near Jericho. | Reorients Joshua: the divine cause is not reducible to Joshua's side. Identity must remain textually cautious. | Joshua 5:13–15 |
| Canaanite kings | Opposing rulers in the conquest accounts. | Provide campaign context; art must avoid racial caricature or spectacle of suffering. | Joshua 9–12 |
