# Legacy

## Scripture-grounded legacy
- **Courage under commission:** “strong and courageous” is tied to God's presence and careful obedience (Deuteronomy 31:7–8; Joshua 1:6–9).
- **Faith:** Joshua and Caleb trust the LORD against the fearful report of the other scouts (Numbers 14:6–9,30).
- **Received leadership:** Joshua succeeds Moses through public commissioning; his wisdom and authority are gifts and responsibilities, not self-appointment (Numbers 27:18–23; Deuteronomy 34:9).
- **Military leadership under God:** Joshua commands real campaigns, but the narrative repeatedly locates victory with the LORD (Joshua 6:2; 10:8–14,42; 11:6–8).
- **Covenant obedience and renewal:** altar, law reading, farewell warning, and Shechem renewal prevent conquest from becoming his sole identity (Joshua 8:30–35; 23–24).
- **Inheritance:** Joshua oversees tribal allotments and accepts his own inheritance after the tribes receive theirs (Joshua 13–21; 19:49–51).
- **Lasting influence:** Israel serves the LORD during Joshua's lifetime and that of the elders who outlive him (Joshua 24:31; Judges 2:7).

## Responsible synthesis
Joshua is best represented as an obedient covenant commander: decisive in action, dependent on God, formed under Moses, responsible to the law, and concerned with the people's inheritance. His failures and limits—Gibeonite counsel not sought, covenant crisis at Ai, unfinished possession, and no ultimate rest—keep the portrait from becoming triumphalist (Joshua 7; 9:14; 13:1; Hebrews 4:8).

## Project interpretation
“The Conqueror,” Battle Commander, and territory-control gameplay compress several themes into a usable card identity. They must always preserve divine agency, covenant accountability, Rahab's rescue, and the land-allotment/covenant-renewal dimensions of Joshua's story.
