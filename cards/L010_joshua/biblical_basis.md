## Major themes and events

### Leadership and faithfulness
Joshua's authority is received: Moses commissions him before Eleazar and the congregation, and the LORD charges him after Moses' death (Numbers 27:18–23; Joshua 1:1–9). Courage is joined to obedience to the law, not detached bravado (Joshua 1:7–8). Joshua and Caleb trust the LORD when the other scouts and congregation rebel (Numbers 14:6–9).

### Jordan crossing
Israel crosses on dry ground while the priests bearing the ark stand in the Jordan. Twelve stones serve as a memorial so future children may ask about the crossing (Joshua 3:1–4:24). The ark signifies covenant presence; it is not Joshua's weapon or a magical device.

### Jericho
Israel follows an unusual command involving armed men, priests, trumpets, the ark, and seven days of procession. At the shout, the wall falls; Rahab and her household are spared according to the spies' oath (Joshua 6). The text attributes the city to the LORD's gift (Joshua 6:2), so art and gameplay must not imply Joshua personally performs magic.

### Covenant and land
After Ai, Joshua builds an altar, writes a copy of the law, and reads blessing and curse before the assembly (Joshua 8:30–35). Land is allotted among the tribes (Joshua 13–21). Joshua's final address warns against idolatry and calls Israel to serve the LORD; covenant is renewed at Shechem (Joshua 23–24).

### Death
Joshua dies at 110 and is buried in his inheritance at Timnath-serah. Israel serves the LORD throughout Joshua's days and those of elders who outlive him (Joshua 24:29–31).

## Interpretive controls
- The book reports conquest with summary statements and also territory remaining (Joshua 11:16–23; 13:1–7). Avoid simplistic claims beyond the text.
- Joshua 5:13–15 does not name the commander of the LORD's army as Joshua. Do not merge them visually.
- Hebrews 4:8 names Joshua while arguing that Joshua did not provide the final, ultimate rest. Do not use the verse as an unqualified claim of complete spiritual rest.
- The moral and theological treatment of conquest requires review under the [Theology and Content Guide](../../knowledge/THEOLOGY_AND_CONTENT_GUIDE.md); celebratory violence, ethnic caricature, and dehumanized enemies are prohibited project choices.
